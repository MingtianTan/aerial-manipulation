π0.5, fine-tuned on our demonstrations, unlatches a door through its handle and swings it open. Left: side view. Right: the two cameras the policy sees. 2× speed.

Aerial manipulation data is scarce, but vision-language-action models already know how to use an arm. We keep one, π0.5, for the arm and train a small Flight DiT for flight. On AM-Bench's fully actuated hexarotor we fixed the scripted specialists, recorded 4,800 verified demonstrations and fine-tuned π0.5 to 81 % success with the benchmark's own evaluator (49 % in the AM-Bench paper). Three tests of the Flight DiT so far: at best it ties π0.5 alone.

π0.5 after fine-tuning: one successful rollout per task, default physics, real time. Toss ball has none: the policy carries the ball to the bin and never lets go.

Two models, one vehicle

Retraining a VLA on scarce flight data would spend it on what the VLA already knows. So π0.5 keeps the arm, and a small Flight DiT learns how the vehicle flies: it forecasts where the base will be and hands that to π0.5 as 11 tokens.

Twelve tasks, 4,800 demonstrations

AM-Bench's fully actuated hexarotor with a 4-DoF arm, in Isaac Lab. We first fixed the benchmark's scripted specialists, then let them record verified demonstrations under five physics conditions.

Hover or tap to play the specialist. Badges: successes in 5 runs on default physics, AM-Bench's code → ours (44 of 60 → 60 of 60). * passes only the old check, by pushing the door; † 6 of 8 over all official runs.

π0.5 against ACT and Diffusion Policy

All three learn from the same 960 default-physics demonstrations and are judged with one protocol, 30 rollouts per task and condition. π0.5 leads on default physics; under thrust limits and wind, which none of them saw in training, it drops the most.

Putting the Flight DiT to use

π0.5 alone already succeeds in 82 % of the rollouts on default physics. Three tests have wired the Flight DiT's forecast in differently; none beats π0.5 alone yet.

Test 3 under wind, task by task

  • Moving the base under a frozen π0.5 costs successes, whatever the forecast: in test 2 even an IK replay that learns nothing loses the inspection task.
  • Retraining π0.5 removes the loss (test 3), but the tokens themselves make no measurable difference.
  • Under wind the full system is ahead (157 vs 148, 128 vs 112) and crashes less (22 vs 30, 64 vs 77), below the bar fixed in advance (18 rollouts and p < 0.05).
