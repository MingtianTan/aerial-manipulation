π0.5, fine-tuned on our demonstrations, unlatches a door by its handle and swings it open. Left: side view. Right: the two cameras the policy sees. 2× speed.

Aerial manipulation data is scarce, but vision-language-action models already know how to use an arm. We keep one, π0.5, for the arm and train a small flow-matching model, FlightFlow, that forecasts how the vehicle flies.

Steps 1–3 build the data and the arm policy; steps 4–5 add FlightFlow, which so far ties π0.5 alone on default physics and makes no measurable difference under thrust limits or wind. All numbers are simulation results; each step has its figure below.

How the two models share one vehicle

π0.5 plans the hand and an inverse-kinematics solver places the base. FlightFlow forecasts where the base will fly; we tried three ways of connecting it to π0.5.

The data: fixed experts, 4,400 verified demonstrations

π0.5 learns from scripted experts, so an expert that fails, or passes a loose check by cheating, goes straight into the data. Six of the 11 AM-Bench tasks below got a new expert, a stricter success check or a fixed reset.

Adding FlightFlow: from 13 points behind to level

Three ways to connect FlightFlow and π0.5, each against π0.5 alone on the same seeds.

Test 1 · FlightFlow reads π0.5

How it works

π0.5’s plan, its next 50 end-effector targets, goes into FlightFlow. FlightFlow’s forecast pulls the base. π0.5 is frozen and never sees it.

Offline version · 13–16 Nov

FlightFlow trained on the scripted experts’ demonstrations.

68.8 % π0.5 alone: 82.1 % · without the plan input: 69.4 % · 330 rollouts

Online version · 17 Nov

FlightFlow fine-tuned on π0.5’s own flights. Four tasks.

inspection 0 %, lemon 50 % π0.5 alone: 100 % and 93 % · 30 rollouts per task

  • Moving the base under a frozen π0.5 costs successes, whatever moves it: even solving the IK along π0.5’s own plan, which learns nothing, loses the inspection task.

Test 2 · π0.5 reads FlightFlow

How it works

FlightFlow’s forecast, ten waypoints and its latent z, goes into π0.5’s action expert as 11 tokens. FlightFlow flies the base. The action expert is retrained on 880 demonstrations flown that way.

Default physics · 18–19 Nov

All 11 tasks.

81.8 % π0.5 alone: 82.1 % · 330 rollouts

Aero and wind · 19–20 Nov

Four conditions not seen in training.

no measurable difference in any of the four · 330 rollouts each

  • Retraining π0.5 on demonstrations flown the same way removes the loss; the tokens themselves make no measurable difference.

Test 3 · π0.5 reads FlightFlow’s hidden states

How it works

As test 2, but in place of the 11 tokens π0.5 reads 44 of FlightFlow’s hidden states, through a 3-layer adapter.
